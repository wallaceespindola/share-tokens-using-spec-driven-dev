# Banner design: Ledger Light

The article banners share one visual language. This note records it so new banners stay in the family.

## Philosophy

Ledger Light treats capacity as something you can see. A field is built from small, equal units, each one either lit or spent, arranged on a strict grid in deep space. Nothing is decorative: every cell is a measurement, and the image is the reading of an instrument rather than an illustration. The work should feel meticulously crafted, the product of an engineer-draughtsman's patient hand, where each unit has been placed and weighed.

Space is dark, quiet and deep, a near-black field with a slow gradient and a fine dot lattice that suggests coordinates without drawing them. Form comes from two bodies of units held apart: one drained, one full. Between them runs a single hairline route, and on it travels one small object. The tension between the two bodies, and the thin line that bridges them, carries the whole idea. Composition is asymmetric: the left third stays open and calm, the right two thirds hold the instrument.

Color is a limited, deliberate palette: one luminous accent and one cooler companion on a dark ground. Light is the material. Lit units glow softly, as if lit from inside; spent units are only outlines, barely there. Glow is calibrated with painstaking attention so that nothing blooms into noise and every edge stays crisp at thumbnail size.

Rhythm comes from repetition. The units repeat at one exact pitch, crop marks frame the instrument at its corners, and a dotted return path echoes the main route at half the weight. Scale is quiet: the units are small, the gaps are equal, and the one travelling object is the only thing allowed to break the grid.

Text is rare and thin. At most four words sit on the open left side in a light, technical face, with a single short accent rule. Tiny monospaced reference markers, like the labels on a laboratory plate, name the parts of the instrument. Every word is placed with master-level care; nothing touches, nothing overlaps, nothing falls off the edge.

## How the banners vary

Each platform gets the same instrument with its own palette, seed and words, so the series reads as one family that looks native on each site. The banners are rendered at each platform's verified size (see [PLATFORM_GUIDELINES.md](PLATFORM_GUIDELINES.md)); InfoQ also gets a JPEG.
